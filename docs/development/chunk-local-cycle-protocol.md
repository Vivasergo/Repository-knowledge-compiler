# Proposed local research and transfer cycle

Status: experimental protocol for the approved small pilot. Not loaded by the
installed Create skill yet. The production master prompt remains unchanged.

## Unit of work

Derive a unit from a consequential behavior, its state owner and dependency
chain. Include entry points, immediate implementations, consumers and relevant
tests. Record external edges, excluded sources and material unchecked scope.
Do not split solely by directory, file count or bytes. Use size to trigger a
narrower behavior boundary when the chain cannot be inspected and checked in
one assignment. A research unit is not a required output file.

For the pilot, a fixed source packet controls the input. This does not establish
that automatic repository partitioning works. Check partitioning separately.

## Research, draft, verify

Inspect the unit read-only. Preserve the original findings before condensation.
Record consequential behavior with its acting condition, result, exception,
writer/consumer, affected data unit and evidence boundary. Record when state is
read across asynchronous operations and what is checked after completion.
Do not infer equivalence merely from a shared helper or similar method names.

Draft a coherent documentation fragment while the local source context is
available. Keep the original immutable and record corrections separately.
Retain material conditions and uncertainty in the actual explanatory text.

A separate source check examines both source-to-finding accuracy and
finding-to-passage retention. Its evidence is the implementation and tests,
not the researcher's confidence or agreement between summaries. Inspect relevant
excluded sources and external edges that could qualify the finding.

For each consequential rule, report the actual passage and source anchors,
what was verified and what remains unchecked. Do not mark a whole topic or ID
range retained without checking its material conditions. A source pointer is
adequate only when it recovers the rule without another broad investigation.

Correct the draft and recheck the changed conditions. In the first pilot allow
one correction cycle; unresolved material findings stop progression. This is
a budget rule for the pilot, not a claim that one correction always suffices.

## Assembly and seams

Assemble verified fragments into canonical documents and task routes. Select
sections or files according to independent maintenance tasks and coherent
reading scope. Avoid file quotas and duplicate explanations.

Keep a small working record of changed passages and unresolved edges. Recheck
any material condition altered by shortening, deduplication or generalization.
The assembly stage cannot confer verified status on a new broader assertion.

Check seams against both sides of the source relationship: producer and
consumer, units and identifiers, conversion boundaries, ordering and failure
outcomes, shared defaults and materially different exceptions. Missing source
on one side leaves that relationship unchecked.

Finish with separate accuracy and task-usefulness QA. Preserve review outputs
and verified dispositions. Distinguish a targeted recheck from a complete new
QA pass. Keep claims within the actual evidence inspected.

## Budget and owner interaction

Bound simultaneously active work and total measured cost. Process units
sequentially when needed; do not require a new worker per function. Replace
the previous global two-researcher assumption only in the experimental flow.

Do not send benchmark answers, expected topics or evaluation reports to any
generator or reviewer participating in creation. Ordinary owners invoke the
skill and answer its normal preflight; note retention and this protocol belong
inside the candidate rather than extra owner instructions.

Protect application code, dependencies, secrets, configuration and existing
worktree changes. Use declared documentation scope and the usual mutation
checks. Pilot success does not authorize merging main or publishing npm.
